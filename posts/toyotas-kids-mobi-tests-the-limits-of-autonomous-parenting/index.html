Toyota's Kids Mobi tests the limits of autonomous parenting

November 19, 2025 · 1 min read

Originally posted on LinkedIn, November 19, 2025.

At the Japan Mobility Show 2025, Toyota unveiled a small capsule called Kids Mobi. It is an autonomous electric vehicle with no driver, designed to take children to preschool or school by themselves.

From the outside, it looks like an oversized toy. Inside, it resembles a miniature playroom, with colorful screens, sensors, and a single seat.

For now, it is only a concept. It imagines a world where children move through public spaces without an adult beside them, relying on electronics, algorithms, and a generous dose of flickering screens before breakfast.

Would I send my child to school alone in this lunchbox on wheels? Consider it a parenting test: bad dad or failing mom.

Kids Mobi looks impressive, but it raises deeper questions. Can convenience and confidence in technology replace the small moment when an adult holds a child's hand on the way to a crosswalk, or gives them one last kiss before they enter school? Or is this a line we are not ready to let autonomy cross?

This miniature toy car may be more than a vehicle concept. It may point to the next stage in the relationship between technology, trust, and parenting.
