A few months ago we introduced Cursor into the team - and since then, something deep has changed.

2025-07-24 · LinkedIn

A few months ago we introduced Cursor into the team - and since then, something deep has changed.

Suddenly I noticed: nobody is waiting for answers anymore.

Developers ask questions about the code - and get an instant answer from the chat.
Why it's built this way. Where the logic is managed. Which tests need to be written.

In the past, every question like that would go straight to the team's Tech lead, or to the most senior person, or to whoever wrote the module.

Today - they talk to the chat, and in the vast majority of cases it knows how to answer and explain.
And that changes everything.

Cursor doesn't just shorten processes - it releases dependency.
It gives developers freedom of movement inside the code, and outside it too.
They ask without apologizing, understand without help, and move forward at their own pace.

More than once I've dived into neighboring repos (the DevOps ones, for example, or shared code libraries) and wasn't afraid to write and change things - thanks to Cursor.

And on the other side - the veterans on the team suddenly aren't required to answer everything either. They get their time back. Valuable time that translates into a higher development pace than ever.

There's a cumulative effect here:
The asker learns. The veteran is freed up. The whole team moves faster.
PRs get merged faster. Tasks get finished. What do I mean finished - wiped out!

Product and design feel it too: the demands go up, the feedback shortens, the pressure shifts to them in some sense.

It's hard to put a number on it, but the feeling is clear: a team that adopted Cursor deeply - works differently.

If you have questions or want to know more about using Cursor or adopting it in a team - I'd love to hear! 💡
