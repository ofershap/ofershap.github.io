Title: How do you turn a year of personal writing into a talk with an agent?

**Short answer:** Collect the raw material into one file, write a brief with audience, goal, and time, ask for an outline before slides, and check every claim against the source before approving a slide. The agent is excellent at synthesizing existing material, and the responsibility for accuracy stays with you.

### Collect the material before asking for output

My agent got a file with a year of my LinkedIn posts. That's what allowed it to find a real line of thinking instead of a generic deck about AI. Without your own material, you'll get a talk anyone could give.

Example: one tidy file sorted by date, with a link to every source. Thirty full posts beat 200 summary lines.

### Write a brief of audience, goal, and time

"Prepare a talk for me" returns something generic. A brief that works answers three questions: who sits in the audience, what should they do differently after the talk, and how many minutes you have. Mine was small business owners, 45 minutes, and one goal: that they go home with a first task they can give an agent.

### Ask for an outline before slides

The cheapest time to fix a talk is before it's a deck. Ask for an outline first: an arc, a core message per part, and at least one demo. After the outline is approved, ask for slides and a prep guide.

A prep guide is worth no less than the slides: what to say on each slide, how much time per part, and where to stop for questions.

### Check every claim against the source

The agent phrases confidently even when it's half-accurate. Every number, date, or tool name on a slide needs backing: a link to the source in the raw material, or a current official document. An unsupported claim leaves the deck.

Example: if a slide says "companies adopting agents save 30% of development time" and there's no source, replace it with data you can show, or with a sentence about your own experience only. Anthropic's long-context prompting guidelines also recommend giving concrete examples and asking for a quote from the source before synthesis, which is exactly this check.

### Prepare a short emergency version

Half an hour before the conference, things break: the projector, the power, the network. Keep a ten-minute version: three points, one example, one message. If everything collapses, you still deliver value.
